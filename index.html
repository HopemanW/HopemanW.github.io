Economics, computation,

I am an Economics Ph.D. candidate at The Ohio State University, with fields in Industrial Organization and Financial Economics. My work focuses on competition, financial intermediation, strategic interaction, and the allocation of scarce resources.

Methodologically, I work with structural estimation, simulation, optimization, dynamic models, causal inference, latent-state models, neural networks, and network analysis. I am especially interested in using machine learning to make high-dimensional economic models more tractable without losing economic structure or interpretability.

01
Banking · Structural IO · Machine Learning

Corporate Lending, Syndicate Formation, and Bank Competition

A structural model of lead-bank retention, participant-bank demand, syndicate formation, tranche allocation, and loan pricing under information frictions. The estimation strategy combines EM-style latent-state methods, neural-network approximations, and bank-network features to study how lending relationships shape credit allocation and pricing.

02
Industrial Organization · Transportation · Dynamic Models

Competition and Capacity Sharing in International Shipping

Empirical and structural analysis of carrier pricing, route choice, market power, and capacity-sharing agreements in international shipping. I estimate differentiated-product demand and multi-product supply, recover marginal costs and markups, and run counterfactual simulations under alternative competitive regimes, with dynamic extensions using neural-network value-function approximation.
